## 1. Executive summary

1. **There are three live definitions of "the score", plus a stronger shadow one.** Actions/gates use the legacy composite, the scoreboard ranks by a hard-coded `*_EVIDENCE_WEIGHTS` table, the "v3" yaml groups are a third view, and the peer-relative decision-domain layer (the best method in the repo) is switched off (`rank_cutover.enabled: false`). Tuning (#262) can reach only some of them. (A-01..A-03)
2. **Price strength dominates and is partly counted twice.** Measured: `relative_strength_60d` ranks identically to `momentum_60d` on every date (Spearman 1.000), momentum vs trend ρ≈0.8, momentum vs RS ρ≈0.75. The legacy action composite is 75% price strength once unavailable models are redistributed; the headline is 43% (stock) / 54% (ETF), and because binary trend has the widest spread it carries about 28% (stock) / 44% (ETF) of the independent variance. (B-01..B-05)
3. **A score can be produced from one input.** `combine_component_scores` has no minimum coverage: a certificate with only a liquidity input got `evidence_score_10 = 10.0` (rank 1 in the sort); 78 of 81 instruments are labelled `low_quality_manual_review`, so the label does not discriminate. (A-04, H-01)
4. **Value and quality are mostly absolute, not sector-relative.** 3 of 5 value inputs are fixed 0–10% yield ladders; all 6 quality inputs are fixed anchors; only P/E vs peers is relative, and the peer sets exist only for the 17 "Industrials" in the 25-stock universe. (D-01, C-01)
5. **Stock-style scoring is applied to non-bank financials and ETFs have no product-quality term.** `BRK_B`, `DB1` and `ENX` get the generic stock score; TER, tracking difference, premium/discount and tax are absent from the headline ETF score (the universe has `ter: None`), and a written KID cost helper is never called. (F-02, G-01..G-03)
6. **No FX conversion in the price features.** 25 of 59 instruments are quoted in NOK/USD/GBP/SEK, so momentum, relative strength and risk are in local currency for a EUR investor. (B-06)
7. **Validation is under-powered for what #811 must show** (§12): t≥3 needs a mean IC of ≈0.055–0.10 even with 5 years of prices; fundamentals PIT depth is unknown. Neutrality covers sector and size only, and the neutral IC is computed against un-neutralised returns. (K-01..K-03)
8. **Statistical robustness (owner addition)** is missing in all four requested forms; only the decision layer has rank shrinkage and bootstrap intervals (§15).

## 14. What 0–10 means per asset class (feeds #941)

- **Stock (today):** a weighted mean of cross-sectional robust z-scores (price; 5 = median of today's mixed universe) and fixed-anchor fundamentals/liquidity ladders. It is neither a rank nor an absolute assessment. **Proposed:** the 0–10 shown is the stock's percentile (÷10) of `0.5·underwriting z + 0.5·valuation z` within its asset-class/sector cohort (bands: ≥9 High, ≥7.5 Attractive, 4–7.5 Fair/Mixed, 2.5–4 Low, <2.5 Unattractive — from `decision_opportunity_v1.yaml`), shown with coverage and an uncertainty band; tactical price strength is a separate timing label.
- **ETF (today):** the same price-strength-dominated mean plus concentration and absolute turnover; no product-quality term. **Proposed:** two numbers: *product quality* (cost, tracking, liquidity, structure, tax, currency) = percentile within the same exposure/benchmark group; *exposure attractiveness* = percentile of look-through valuation/quality vs exposure peers. Never one composite, and an 8/10 ETF is a percentile claim within its group, not comparable to a stock 8/10.
- **Sparebank EC (today):** an absolute underwriting rating: linear interpolation between book-anchored (provisional) thresholds with caps at 3 (regulatory floor breaches) and 5 (days-to-trade >20). **Proposed:** keep absolute; label it "underwriting rating, multi-year, not comparable to relative scores"; never merge it with the stock rank without a horizon label (F-04).
